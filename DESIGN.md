# Deepwick: design direction

A light-survival mining game. Not a Terraria clone: the thing you are really
gathering, spending and defending is **light**.

## The pitch

The mining town of Deepwick lived around the Wick, a great lantern whose flame
kept the dark in the mountain. Something far below (the Gloamheart) has been
drinking the light. The Wick is cold, the town is empty, and at night the Gloam
rolls down the valley.

You wake beside the cold Wick with nothing. Every trip underground is an
expedition into the dark: your lantern oil is your clock, darkness wears you
down, and what you bring back up (ore for tools, and anything that glows for
the flame) decides how far the light reaches tomorrow.

## Pillars

1. **Light is life.** Being in the dark is the danger, not just the enemies in
   it. Light sources you place stay lit and make the mountain safer over time.
2. **The Wick is the heart.** One hub to feed and grow. Its flame level drives
   progression: the safe radius at night, which settlers come back, what they
   offer. No abstract deeds.
3. **Expeditions, not just digging.** Push down, light a waypoint, push again,
   get home before the oil runs out. Depth is gated by light and nerve as much
   as by pickaxe tier.
4. **Readable and welcoming.** A clear next goal on screen at all times,
   difficulty choices, controller support, and juicy feedback.

## Core loop

- **Day, surface:** craft, cook, fish, trade, feed the Wick.
- **Descent:** mine ore and collect glowing things (glowcaps, jade, amethyst,
  lumenite, glowfin...). Plant Wickposts to make lit, safe waypoints you can
  travel back to.
- **Return:** refine glow into Lumen at the Wick, grow the flame, unlock the
  next settler or upgrade, forge better tools and lanterns.
- **Night:** the Gloam comes down the valley. Inside the Wick's light you are
  safe; outside it the Hollows hunt.

## Roadmap (one review cycle each, every cycle ends shippable)

- [x] Cycle 1: empty-handed miner; tools drawn in hand only when held and swung
      (each pick tier looks different). Playthrough bug fixes.
- [x] Cycle 2: the Wick. Kindle it (15 wood, 3 coal), feed it Lumen from
      anything that glows, five flame levels. Settlers arrive by flame level
      (deeds removed; old saves migrate), nothing hostile survives in its light,
      a warm safe pool at night, rest heals faster there. Wickposts at flame 2:
      lit waypoints, travel between any of them and the Wick.
- [x] Cycle 3: darkness. Nerve (0-100, saved) drains in the dark (faster the
      deeper you go, much faster in the Sunless Vault, halved by the Sunglass
      Lens) and recovers in light, fastest by a campfire or in the Wick's
      light. Frayed under 60: the screen closes in. Shaken under 35: a
      heartbeat, whispers, tendrils of dark at the screen edge, and Gloamlings
      hunt you while you stand in the dark. At 0 the dark bites. Gloamlings are
      living shadow: they seep through rock, circle at the edge of your light,
      burn in it, and drop Gloam Motes (6 Lumen). Difficulty: Hearth (keep your
      pack, softer), Delver, Gloam (harder, more Gloamlings); picked on the
      intro card, changeable from the pause menu.
- [x] Cycle 4: guidance. The Journal: 21 goals in four chapters (The Cold
      Wick, Into the Dark, The Long Descent, The Deep Places) walking the whole
      loop from punching wood to stilling the Gloamheart. Each is judged from the
      world state, pays coins and Delving XP, shows on the HUD under the minimap
      with a progress bar, and in full with how-to text on J. Old saves skip
      what they have done without being paid twice. Controller support: stick
      movement, right-stick aim (idle aim works the block in front of you, or
      below while holding down), triggers to use, an on-screen pointer that
      clicks through every menu and the title, button latching across menu
      changes, and a controller section in the pause menu.
- [x] Cycle 5: Gloam Tides. Every fourth night from the third (once the Wick
      is Glowing) the dark rises against the town: a bruised sky and a red
      moon, the Wick gutters to half its reach, and tide-born Gloamlings come
      over the hills from both sides to gnaw at its roots, draining its ward
      (HUD bar). The guttering Wick cannot burn them, but torches and lamps on
      the approaches can, so the town wants a lit perimeter. Hold until dawn
      for Lumen and coins (scaled by the ward left and the tide cut down); let
      the ward break and the Gloam eats some of the Lumen toward the next flame
      (never a whole level). No sleeping through a tide; a dawn warning and a
      "Tide tonight" chip; its own siege music (PixelLab). Journal goal added.
- [x] Cycle 6: the Wick catches. Kindling (and every later flame level) is now an event: the light spreads out from the glass over 1.5s instead of snapping on, rings of light race to the new edge, slow rays turn about the lamp, a flash and a centred title card ("THE WICK BURNS / Deepwick has a heart again."), plus a new kindle sound. The two old flame-up toasts are gone. Accessibility & options in the pause menu: screen shake & flashes on/off (also softens the hurt flash), tutorial hints on/off (the Journal still reads the hint flags), HUD size 85-130%, fullscreen; saved with the volume settings. Measured the opening: bare hands fell a tree in ~3.7s, five trees within 30 tiles, coal ~11m down, so kindling takes ~3 minutes, well before the first night (~6 minutes); no pacing change needed.
- [x] Cycle 7: bring them home. The smith and the archivist no longer just appear: when the Wick grows Glowing (and Bright) its light is seen from below, and they answer from a hollow in the rock by a fire burning low (Brannoc 34-44m down in the Topsoil Tunnels, Wren 72-86m down in the Mossroot Grotto). A compass under the Journal and a blinking marker on both maps point the way; they call out when you are near. Talk to them and they follow you home along the exact path you walked (walk cycles from PixelLab), carrying a little light, chatting in speech bubbles, and halving the nerve the dark costs you. Wickposts and recall bring them along; black out and they go back to their fire. Inside the Wick's light their house goes up, they walk over to their door, and a title card welcomes them home. Two Journal goals added, and the Journal now remembers goals by id so new ones never shift a save. Title cards queue instead of overwriting each other.
- [x] Cycle 8: Hesper's Stillroom. A fourth townsperson: at the Blazing flame Hesper the apothecary answers from the Crystal Hollows (150-168m down), the deepest rescue yet, with her own PixelLab portrait, walk cycle and an open-fronted stillroom with a copper still. Home, she brews from coin and what you carry up: Steady Draught (2 glowcaps: +60 nerve at once), Nightglass Tonic (2 jade: three minutes of wider sight in the dark and half the nerve drain), Gloamward Salve (3 Gloam Motes: three minutes in which no Gloamling hunts you or rises near you and the tide-born pass you by). Active brews show as a timed chip under the Wick chip. New Journal goal; older saves that are already past a rescue page see it as still to do. Verified saving and reloading mid-escort (the follower resumes at your heels).
- [x] Cycle 9: readable at a glance. Notifications rebuilt so the top of the screen never stacks five banners: tutorial tips have their own card bottom-left (one at a time, queued, click to dismiss, hidden while a window is open); Journal pages tick off inside the goal tracker itself (green, with the coins) and turn in order; chapters and the opening ("DEEPWICK / You wake beside the Wick...") are title cards; level-ups light up the skill row (perk in its tooltip) instead of a toast; news toasts are compact, at most three, and a repeat counts up (x3) instead of stacking. Pickups of the same thing merge into one counting label (+5 Wood). Numbers were hard to read: Pixelify Sans draws 5 like an S (1,500 read as 1,800), so every digit in the HUD, menus and in-world text now comes from Silkscreen. Title cards no longer wait 2.6s after a page load.
- [x] Cycle 10: phones and tablets. Deepwick now plays by touch. A thumbstick appears wherever the left thumb lands (push up to climb, down to drop); Jump, Use, Pack and Oil sit under the right thumb, with a pause button at the top. A finger on the world is the mouse: hold it on a block to dig, a tree to chop, an enemy to fight; tap empty ground to place or eat what you hold; tap a door, bed, chest, Wickpost or station to open it. A still finger stays on the spot it touched even as the camera leans with your facing. The Use button glows when there is something to talk to or travel with. In the pack, holding a stack splits it and a double tap quick-moves or equips it. Tips speak touch ("tap Use") once you touch the screen, and the pause menu lists the controls you are using first. Short screens get a compact HUD (smaller bars and minimap, skills hidden, narrower hotbar that clears both thumbs), the intro card fits a phone, panels use the whole screen, and a phone held upright asks to be turned. Starting a game on a phone goes fullscreen and locks landscape where the browser allows. Installable: a web app manifest and Wick icons, so "Add to Home Screen" opens it fullscreen in landscape. Tapping the Journal goal or the minimap opens them (with a mouse too).
- [x] Cycle 11: smooth on phones. Profiled a phone-sized screen with the CPU slowed 4x: frames took ~20ms, almost all of it one call that blew the 844x390 picture up to 2532x1170 every frame, from a scene canvas that was also forced into software drawing (it had been made "read-frequently"). The renderer now draws straight into the on-screen canvas at one canvas pixel per art pixel and the browser scales it up by a whole number, crisp; sharp text (numbers, speech, prompts) goes on a full-resolution overlay that only wipes the area it drew last frame and fades with the scene. Same picture, main-thread draw time 16ms -> under 1ms, frames 20ms -> ~4ms in the same test, and a much smaller screen-sized buffer to fill. The game now saves whenever it is hidden (switching apps, locking the phone, closing the tab), since phones rarely send a goodbye, and its sound sleeps while hidden and wakes on return or the next touch (including after Safari's interruptions).
- [x] Cycle 12: firedamp and the canary. A mining hazard of its own: sweet, lighter-than-air mine gas sits sealed in pockets in the rock (often ringed with coal) and hangs under the roofs of some caves, from about 22m down through the Ember Depths. Break into a pocket and it hisses out, creeps up to the ceiling and along the tunnel as a pale drifting haze you can see wherever there is light. An open flame in it (a torch in your hand or on a wall, a campfire, a lamp, a furnace, lava, a bomb) sets off the whole connected cloud in a flash that rushes through it tile by tile: it burns whoever stands in it (harder the more gas there was; the first burn is softened), cracks the soft rock around dense gas, and shakes the screen; the lantern is a safety lamp and never lights it. Breathing thick gas wears your nerve down. Old Pell sells a canary (60 coins) that rides in a little cage on your back and smells gas through rock before you can see it: it sings calmly, chirps faster when it is uneasy, flaps and shrieks when gas is close, and faints if you stand in thick gas (HUD chip and the bird on your back show which). Only the area around you is simulated, a few times a second (under 0.1ms a step). Old saves open their seams where the rock is untouched and never beside a fire the player has lit. Also fixed across the whole game: the pixel font drew "fi" and "fl" as a capital A ("Arst", "Aame"); ligatures are now off.
- [x] Cycle 13: bad ground. The second mine hazard after firedamp: some rock is cracked and loose (patches through the Topsoil Tunnels from 14m down to the Ember Depths, a halo around rich ore, and the roofs of the old mine drifts). It stays put only while something holds it up. Dig out the tile under it, or knock out the timber that props it, and it creaks, sheds dust and shudders for about a second, then drops: falling rock hurts whatever it lands on (harder the deeper you are), crushes torches, ladders and rails, and piles up as gravel you dig through by hand. What rested on it follows close behind, so a whole roof can come down in a chain and wall you in. Ore and glowing rock break into what you would have mined. A rock that lands in firedamp can strike a spark and set it off. Timber Props (3 wood makes 2, by hand; Old Pell sells them too) stand on a floor and hold the roof over them two tiles either side; the cap board along the roof shows exactly what a prop holds, in the placement preview too. The old drift frames are props now (they drop one when broken), and the bad roofs over them come down if you take them. Hover corners turn amber over anything whose removal would free loose rock. New PixelLab creak and rockfall sounds, a hand-drawn prop icon. Saves keep loose rock; older worlds get it only where the rock is untouched and held.
- [x] Cycle 14: floodwater, the third mine hazard. Sealed pockets of water sit in the rock (about 22 a world, 19-49 tiles each: a few in the Topsoil Tunnels below 22m, most in the wet Mossroot Grotto, some in the Crystal Hollows). Rock with water close behind it weeps: it looks dark and wet, with seep streaks and glints, and its underside drips far more than dry rock, so a careful delver can tell. Break into a pocket and it bursts out with a gush and a spray and runs down into whatever you dug below it; a tunnel or pit can fill over your head. Under water you hold your breath: five bubbles over your head, two seconds each, flashing red on the last; then you drown (5 health a second, and your nerve goes too), and you gasp when you come up. Fixed a long-standing water bug on the way: water draining through a one-tile gap stalled after a tile or two, because the water waiting behind was never woken again (now the row is). New PixelLab gush and gasp sounds. Older worlds get the same pockets where the rock around them is still whole.
- [x] Cycle 15: the delver's trade (review and integration). The three hazards now sit in the loop instead of beside it. The Journal teaches them: "Carry a canary" (Into the Dark, after 30m) and "Shore up bad ground" (The Long Descent, after the first copper bar); saves already past them skip them. Floodwater pays: a sealed pocket that has never been broken into washes out 1-3 Cave Pearls (15 Lumen each, a new hand-drawn icon), once per pocket and remembered in the save, so weeping rock is worth the risk. A fresh-eyes code review of the hazard systems found, and this cycle fixed: old saves could pour a water pocket or release a gas seam straight into a tunnel the player had already dug through it (each pocket and seam is now judged whole, with the rock round it, and left alone if touched; roof gas near where you stood or by your fires is cleared); you could wake at a bed under floodwater (you now wake at the meadow); a rock landing in water deleted that water (it now shatters), and rubble no longer lands inside a creature; a large open lake could set off the "water bursts" alarm; gas piled up against the top of the simulated area like an invisible ceiling; and a flash could run through stale gas outside open air.
- [x] Cycle 16: the Lampless. A creature built on the game's own rule, light, instead of another walker with more health. From the Crystal Hollows down walk the Lampless, miners who went down and never came back (a PixelLab sprite: a pale, hunched figure in a dented hard hat with a dead head-lamp, with a lurching walk and a cowering pose). Light holds one rooted where it stands, arm over its eyes, trembling; in the dark it comes for you fast, climbs walls and the sides of shafts, and if it cannot get through it steps out of the dark somewhere else in your cave. Near one, your own flame (lantern or torch in hand) sputters and then gutters low for a moment, and the cave sinks into darkness: that is when it lurches closer, and on the next gutter it reaches you (a hard hit and a cold touch on your nerve). You hear it before you see it: dragging footsteps when it moves, a moan when your light first falls on it. Torches on the wall never gutter, so a torch pins one for good (and a pinned one stops leaning on your flame); strike it while it cowers and it is laid to rest in a drift of pale dust, leaving its Cold Lamp (hand-drawn icon). Bring the lamp home and the Wick lights it again (20 Lumen) and says whose it was: Tamsin Hale, Old Corran, Pell's brother Arno and twenty more, named in order. They are met now and then (45-90 seconds apart, one at a time; two in the Sunless Vault, where they are tougher and hit harder), never in the Wick's light. New Journal page "Lay a Lampless to rest" after reaching the Hollows; three PixelLab sounds (gutter, lurch, moan). With screen flashes off the sputter is a steady dip instead of a flicker.
- [x] Cycle 17: the Gloamheart rebuilt around light. The old bullet-pattern boss and its sprite are gone. The heart now hangs in a shroud of living dark that drinks your lamp oil, and nothing touches it there. Six old sconces stand round the chamber (E to light): 2, then 4, then 6 burning at once tear the shroud away and bring it down to the floor, open for a few seconds, where every hit refills a little oil. Each phase has a damage floor; reaching it, the heart rises and drinks every sconce dry. While shrouded it sends Gloamling raiders to snuff the sconces, reaches for you with telegraphed tendrils that can't strike inside sconce light, makes your flame gutter from phase 2, and calls Lampless in the last phase. Torches you place in the arena are drunk (and dropped back to you). The chamber has no torches of its own any more. New PixelLab heart, sconce, and drink / tendril / ignite sounds. The boss bar now appears once the title card clears, so the two never overlap on phones.
- [x] Cycle 18: Dawn over Deepwick, a real ending. The old ending was a stats box that said the light poured back up through the caves while nothing in the world changed. Now, when the Gloamheart is stilled, the HUD steps aside, the music holds its breath, and the light it drank gathers into one bright point and climbs straight up through the mountain. The camera rides it past every place you went down through, each named as it goes by with the depth counting down (Heart Chamber, Sunless Vault, Ember Depths, Crystal Hollows, Mossroot Grotto, Topsoil Tunnels, Deepwick), and every torch, lamp, Wickpost and campfire you left on the way flares as it passes. It pours into the Wick's glass under a night sky; the Wick takes it ("Dawn over Deepwick"), the sun comes up over the town in a few seconds, and the light carries you home beside the Wick, whole. Then an epilogue: a PixelLab illustration of the town at dawn, who came home (Pell, Brannoc, Wren, Hesper, or where each still waits), the lost miners whose lamps you carried up, named one by one, and your run. The world stays changed: the Wick burns at Dawn (reach 26, whiter, its own name in the HUD and panel), the Gloam Tides are over, Gloamlings no longer rise, the Lampless are at rest, and the dark frays your nerve half as fast. Nothing can hurt you while the light carries you home; a save taken mid-ending wakes you at the Wick with the reward. A new PixelLab dawn theme (two minutes, building from a music box to a full sunrise) and a rising-light sound.
- [x] Cycle 19: the first day (a fresh-eyes review of the opening). A world-generation bug: the old mine drifts were dug at depths measured from their own shaft's hilltop, so where they ran in under the lower meadow they broke out through the grass. In about one new world in twenty (8 of 150 seeds) this opened a hole right where you wake, and the very first thing a new player did was fall into a dark store room under the Wick. In nearly a third of worlds (47 of 150) planked tunnels lay open to the sky somewhere. Drifts and store rooms now stop short of the surface, with at least three tiles of cover: 0 of 150 either way. Old saves keep their own tiles. The first day is now a visible clock: while the Wick is cold the Journal card counts down "Sundown in 5:12" (pulsing in the last 75 s), then "Hold out: dawn in 4:18" through the night, the promise the intro card already made. A minute before dark the old town bell tolls (a new PixelLab sound) every evening; with the Wick cold it warns you to kindle it or get behind a door, and on a tide night it calls you to the Wick. The dusk tip now names the fallback (a campfire and a door) for a delver who will not make it.
- [x] Cycle 20: three worlds. There was one save, and New World replaced it after a confirm box: no trying Gloam mode on the side, no fresh start after the ending without losing your finished world, no sharing a device. Now there are three. A first visit still has a single New World button; after that the title offers Continue (naming the world and its day) and Worlds, a page of three cards. Each world's card shows a strip of its map in section (the valley's layers, faint where you have not been, bright where you have, a gold line at your deepest), its day, the Wick's flame, deepest point, time played, difficulty, whether the heart is stilled and when it was last played, with Play and Delete. An empty slot offers New World. World 1 keeps the old save key, so an existing save simply becomes World 1 and its card is read out of it once. The card is stored apart from the world so the title never parses whole worlds; if storage is tight the map strip is dropped before the save is ever put at risk.
- [x] Cycle 21: weather that fights your light. From the second day on, weather comes down the valley about once a day: rain, storms, and on some mornings fog, easing in and out over twenty seconds and kept in the save. Rain makes a torch or campfire under open sky sputter and burn low, catching and dropping and hissing steam; give it a roof (any block anywhere above it) and it burns bright, so where you build your fires starts to matter. The Wick, lamps and your lantern's glass do not mind the wet, and a torch in your hand burns low only while you stand in the open. A storm greys the sky, rolls a cloud wrack over the peaks and hides the moon and stars; now and then lightning throws the whole valley into white for an instant (the light map really lights up, so you see what the dark was hiding), with a jagged bolt, a near crack or far rumble of thunder after it (muffled if you are just below ground), and on a night out in the open a close one frays your nerve. Fog fills the valley floor with pale drifting banks. Rain has upsides: saplings and crops grow faster and fish bite sooner. Rain and splashes stop at the first solid thing above them (they fall down open shafts and land on the top of a pond), and are lit like everything else, glittering in torchlight and invisible in the dark. A weather word and icon beside the clock, a toast when it turns, a tip the first time. The sky clears as the light climbs home at the end. Three new PixelLab sounds (a seamless rain bed, near and far thunder), shrunk to mono to keep the download small. No measurable frame cost.
